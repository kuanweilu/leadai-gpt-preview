The Year AI Does the Work: What Managers Actually Manage Now

LEADAI · Field noteThe Year AI Does the Work: What Managers Actually Manage NowHuman leads. AI follows.

Sega Cheng calls 2026 the year AI starts doing the work: AI has evolved from Q&A assistant to delegated avatar, and real transformation is not swapping systems or subscribing to tools — it is a change in how decisions are made. He also names where enterprises stall: lagging process integration, painful data preparation, and AI lacking last-mile execution authority. The technology races ahead while organizations live in a parallel world.

That diagnosis explains why Manage (Align the Team) is the most underrated of the four dimensions. Basic Managing means knowing how to verify AI output. Proficient means designing the division of labor across multiple AIs. Advanced means composing an effective human-AI hybrid team — who judges, who executes, who owns the outcome, written down and actually running.

In the year of delegation, managers no longer manage hours. They manage three things: the allocation of judgment, the gatekeeping of quality, and the handling of exceptions. Design these well and AI earns execution authority; design them badly and the smartest model remains an expensive chat window.

Once AI takes over execution, management finally returns to its original meaning: alignment.
